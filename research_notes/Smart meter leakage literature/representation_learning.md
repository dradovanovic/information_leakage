# Representation Learning / Self-Supervised Pretraining for Smart Meter Load Profiles and General Time Series

Scope: encoders usable as a shared, public pretrained initialisation for classifiers on weekly smart meter snippets (7x48 at 30 min, or 7x24 hourly; i.e. univariate length 336 or 168, or a 7-channel/2D layout).

## 1. General time-series SSL methods: architectures, use for classification, encoder sizes

### Takeaway
For classification, the standard citable SSL baselines are **TS2Vec** (contrastive, dilated CNN), **TS-TCC / CA-TCC** (contrastive, small CNN + transformer), **TST** (masked reconstruction, transformer), **TF-C** (time-frequency contrastive, built for cross-dataset transfer), **T-Loss** and **TNC**, with **SimMTM** as the main masked-modelling method that also reports classification. **PatchTST**, **CoST** and **Ti-MAE** are mainly forecasting methods. All of these encoders are small (roughly 0.1–2M parameters), have official PyTorch code, and run comfortably on CPU at sequence length 168–336.

### Cited Findings
- **TS2Vec** [K]: Yue, Z., Wang, Y., Duan, J., Yang, T., Huang, C., Tong, Y., Xu, B. "TS2Vec: Towards Universal Representation of Time Series." AAAI 2022. [arXiv:2106.10466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10466); PyTorch: [github.com/yuezhihan/ts2vec](https://github.com/yuezhihan/ts2vec). Encoder: input projection, then timestamp masking, then a dilated CNN of 10 residual blocks (hidden 64, output 320-dim per timestamp). Training uses hierarchical contrastive loss (instance-wise and temporal) over random overlapping crops. For classification, embeddings are max-pooled over time and fed to an RBF-SVM. Evaluated on UCR (128) and UEA (30) archives. The most widely used SSL baseline for TSC.
- **TS-TCC** [K]: Eldele, E., Ragab, M., Chen, Z., Wu, M., Kwoh, C.K., Li, X., Guan, C. "Time-Series Representation Learning via Temporal and Contextual Contrasting." IJCAI 2021. [arXiv:2106.14112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14112); PyTorch: [github.com/emadeldeen24/TS-TCC](https://github.com/emadeldeen24/TS-TCC). Uses a 3-block 1D-CNN encoder with weak (jitter+scale) and strong (permutation+jitter) augmentations. Temporal contrasting uses a small transformer to predict the future of one view from the other, plus contextual (NT-Xent) contrasting. Evaluated on HAR, Sleep-EDF, epilepsy, and fault diagnosis, including few-label fine-tuning.
- **CA-TCC** [K]: Eldele, E. et al. "Self-Supervised Contrastive Representation Learning for Semi-Supervised Time-Series Classification." IEEE TPAMI 45(12), 2023. [arXiv:2208.06616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06616); same PyTorch repo family ([github.com/emadeldeen24/CA-TCC](https://github.com/emadeldeen24/CA-TCC)). Extends TS-TCC with pseudo-label class-aware (supervised) contrastive fine-tuning and reports results at 1%, 5%, 10%, 50%, 75% labels. Directly relevant to label scarcity.
- **TST (masked pretraining)** [K]: Zerveas, G., Jayaraman, S., Patel, D., Bhamidipaty, A., Eickhoff, C. "A Transformer-based Framework for Multivariate Time Series Representation Learning." KDD 2021. [arXiv:2010.02803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02803); DOI [10.1145/3447548.3467401](https://doi.org/10.1145/3447548.3467401); PyTorch: [github.com/gzerveas/mvts_transformer](https://github.com/gzerveas/mvts_transformer). A transformer encoder (typically d_model 64–128, 3 layers, 8 heads, BatchNorm) pretrained by reconstructing masked values. Masks are per-variable segments with geometric lengths (mask ratio 0.15, mean span 3). Fine-tuned for classification and regression (UEA). The paper reports that pretraining helps even without extra unlabeled data, i.e. reusing the same training samples.
- **PatchTST (incl. self-supervised)** [K]: Nie, Y., Nguyen, N.H., Sinthong, P., Kalagnanam, J. "A Time Series is Worth 64 Words: Long-term Forecasting with Transformers." ICLR 2023. [arXiv:2211.14730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730); PyTorch: [github.com/yuqinie98/PatchTST](https://github.com/yuqinie98/PatchTST). Uses channel independence, patching (e.g. patch 16, stride 8) and RevIN. The SSL variant masks about 40% of patches and reconstructs them. The paper evaluates forecasting and cross-dataset transfer, not classification. Hugging Face `transformers` ships `PatchTSTForClassification` and `PatchTSTForPretraining` [K/U], so a masked-patch-pretrained encoder plus a classification head is easy to build in PyTorch.
- **TNC** [K]: Tonekaboni, S., Eytan, D., Goldenberg, A. "Unsupervised Representation Learning for Time Series with Temporal Neighborhood Coding." ICLR 2021. [arXiv:2106.00750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00750); PyTorch: [github.com/sanatonek/TNC_representation_learning](https://github.com/sanatonek/TNC_representation_learning). Uses an ADF-test-defined temporal neighbourhood with positive-unlabeled (debiased) contrastive loss and an RNN/CNN encoder. Designed for non-stationary signals.
- **T-Loss** [K]: Franceschi, J.-Y., Dieuleveut, A., Jaggi, M. "Unsupervised Scalable Representation Learning for Multivariate Time Series." NeurIPS 2019. [arXiv:1901.10738](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10738); PyTorch: [github.com/White-Link/UnsupervisedScalableRepresentationLearningTimeSeries](https://github.com/White-Link/UnsupervisedScalableRepresentationLearningTimeSeries). A causal dilated CNN with global max-pooling, trained with a triplet loss (subseries positives, negatives drawn from other series) and an SVM on top.
- **TF-C** [K]: Zhang, X., Zhao, Z., Tsiligkaridis, T., Zitnik, M. "Self-Supervised Contrastive Pre-Training for Time Series via Time-Frequency Consistency." NeurIPS 2022. [arXiv:2206.08496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08496); PyTorch: [github.com/mims-harvard/TFC-pretraining](https://github.com/mims-harvard/TFC-pretraining). Time and frequency encoders with a time-frequency consistency loss. Explicitly evaluates "pretrain on one dataset (e.g. SleepEEG), fine-tune on a different target" (one-to-many) for classification, which is the closest conceptual match to a shared public pretrained initialisation.
- **CoST** [K]: Woo, G., Liu, C., Sahoo, D., Kumar, A., Hoi, S. "CoST: Contrastive Learning of Disentangled Seasonal-Trend Representations for Time Series Forecasting." ICLR 2022. [arXiv:2202.01575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01575); PyTorch: [github.com/salesforce/CoST](https://github.com/salesforce/CoST). Forecasting-oriented and uses a TS2Vec-style backbone. Its seasonal (frequency-domain) contrast is conceptually attractive for daily/weekly periodicity in load.
- **SimMTM** [K]: Dong, J., Wu, H., Zhang, H., Zhang, L., Wang, J., Long, M. "SimMTM: A Simple Pre-Training Framework for Masked Time-Series Modeling." NeurIPS 2023. [arXiv:2302.00861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00861); PyTorch: [github.com/thuml/SimMTM](https://github.com/thuml/SimMTM). Reconstructs masked series by weighted aggregation of multiple masked neighbours on the series manifold. Reports both forecasting and classification, including in- and cross-domain fine-tuning (SleepEEG to Epilepsy/FD-B/Gesture/EMG, as in TF-C).
- **Ti-MAE** [K/U]: Li, Z., Rao, Z., Pan, L., Wang, P., Xu, Z. "Ti-MAE: Self-Supervised Masked Time Series Autoencoders." arXiv 2023, [arXiv:2301.08871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08871). An MAE-style approach with a high mask ratio. Primarily forecasting, with some classification experiments. Peer-reviewed venue unclear [U].
- **TimesURL** [K]: Liu, J., Chen, S. "TimesURL: Self-supervised Contrastive Learning for Universal Time Series Representation Learning." AAAI 2024. [arXiv:2312.15709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15709). Combines frequency-temporal augmentation, hard negatives and a reconstruction term on a TS2Vec backbone, and evaluates classification among other tasks.
- **Survey (for framing)** [K]: Zhang, K., Wen, Q., Zhang, C., Cai, R., Jin, M., Liu, Y., Zhang, J., Liang, Y., Pang, G., Song, D., Pan, S. "Self-Supervised Learning for Time Series Analysis: Taxonomy, Progress, and Prospects." IEEE TPAMI 2024. [arXiv:2306.10125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10125).

### Inferences
- For 168- or 336-step weekly snippets, TS2Vec (around 0.6M params [U]) and TS-TCC (a small CNN) are the most defensible contrastive baselines. TST or PatchTST-SSL (masked) is the most defensible masked-reconstruction baseline. All are PyTorch and CPU-trainable on CER-scale data (about 4–5k households x about 75 weeks, i.e. a few hundred thousand weekly snippets).
- Augmentation choice matters for load data. Permutation and strong jitter (TS-TCC "strong") can destroy daily or weekly structure that carries household-attribute signal. Cropping/masking (TS2Vec, TST) and conservative perturbations (GridCL, Section 3) are closer to what energy papers use.
- PatchTST-style patching aligns naturally with day boundaries (patch length 48 or 24 = one day), which gives a "7 day-tokens" transformer. This is a design choice, not a published result for this task.

### Gaps
- No standardised encoder-size table across methods was verified; parameter counts above are approximate or unverified.
- No head-to-head benchmark of these SSL methods on smart meter household classification was found.

## 2. Time-series foundation models: classification/embedding support, licenses, sizes, CPU feasibility

### Takeaway
Most large TSFMs (Chronos, TimesFM, Lag-Llama, Moirai, Timer) are **forecasting** models. Embeddings can sometimes be extracted, but classification is not a native, evaluated task. The ones that natively target **classification/embeddings** are **MOMENT** (MIT; small/base/large), **Mantis** (around 8M params, contrastive, built for classification), **UniTS** (multi-task incl. classification), **GPT4TS/OFA**, and IBM **TSPulse** (very small, classification-capable; base of the energy-specific Energy-TSPulse). For CPU feasibility and a "shared public init", Mantis and TSPulse/Energy-TSPulse are the most practical. MOMENT-small/base is feasible but heavier.

### Cited Findings
- **MOMENT** [V]: Goswami, M., Szafer, K., Choudhry, A., Cai, Y., Li, S., Dubrawski, A. "MOMENT: A Family of Open Time-series Foundation Models." ICML 2024. [arXiv:2402.03885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03885). HF cards [AutonLab/MOMENT-1-large](https://huggingface.co/AutonLab/MOMENT-1-large) and [MOMENT-1-base](https://huggingface.co/AutonLab/MOMENT-1-base) list license **MIT** and include a classification task and tutorial [V]. Architecture: T5-based encoder, masked-patch reconstruction pretraining on the "Time-series Pile", patches of 8 observations, RevIN [V via search snippet]. Sizes: small/base/large, approximately 40M/125M/385M parameters [K/U, not confirmed this session]. Fixed input length 512 [K], so a 336-step weekly snippet must be padded (with the mask) or a 168-step one repeated or padded. The paper evaluates unsupervised representations (embeddings + SVM) on UCR classification [K].
- **Mantis** [V]: Feofanov, V. et al. "Mantis: Lightweight Calibrated Foundation Model for User-Friendly Time Series Classification." [arXiv:2502.15637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15637) (Feb 2025). A ViT-style transformer, contrastively pretrained on 7M series, with **8M parameters**. Checkpoint on Hugging Face plus a GitHub package for inference and fine-tuning. Peer-reviewed version: "Mantis: Lightweight Foundation Model for Time Series Classification", ICML 2026, [PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/feofanov26a.html) / [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62438). That version describes pretraining **exclusively on synthetic data**. Follow-up: **MantisV2 / Mantis+**, [arXiv:2602.17868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17868), which claims SOTA zero-shot TSC (HAR, EEG) [V, abstract level]. License not verified this session [U]; PyTorch.
- **Chronos** [K]: Ansari, A.F. et al. "Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series." TMLR 2024. [arXiv:2403.07815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815); [github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting). T5 models tiny–large (about 8M–710M), plus Chronos-Bolt (about 9M–205M) [K]. Apache-2.0 [K]. A forecasting model with value tokenisation (scaling and quantisation); the pipeline exposes encoder embeddings [K/U], but classification is not an evaluated task.
- **TimesFM** [K]: Das, A., Kong, W., Sen, R., Zhou, Y. "A decoder-only foundation model for time-series forecasting." ICML 2024. [arXiv:2310.10688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10688); [github.com/google-research/timesfm](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm). About 200M params (later 2.x releases ~200–500M) [K/U]. Apache-2.0 [K]. Decoder-only and forecasting-only, so it is a poor fit as a classifier init.
- **Lag-Llama** [K]: Rasul, K. et al. "Lag-Llama: Towards Foundation Models for Probabilistic Time Series Forecasting." [arXiv:2310.08278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08278). Small (a few M params), Apache-2.0 [K], univariate probabilistic forecasting. Its pretraining corpus includes energy/load data [K/U].
- **Moirai** [K]: Woo, G., Liu, C., Kumar, A., Xiong, C., Savarese, S., Sahoo, D. "Unified Training of Universal Time Series Forecasting Transformers." ICML 2024. [arXiv:2402.02592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02592). Small/base/large approximately 14M/91M/311M [K/U]. Moirai-1.0 weights are **CC-BY-NC-4.0** [K/U, check the HF card], i.e. non-commercial. The LOTSA pretraining corpus includes BuildingsBench and other energy data [K/U]. Forecasting-only.
- **Timer** [K]: Liu, Y., Zhang, H., Li, C., Huang, X., Wang, J., Long, M. "Timer: Generative Pre-trained Transformers Are Large Time Series Models." ICML 2024. [arXiv:2402.02368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02368). Decoder-only, generative. Tasks: forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection; classification is not central [K/U].
- **UniTS** [K]: Gao, S., Koker, T., Queen, O., Hartvigsen, T., Tsiligkaridis, T., Zitnik, M. "UniTS: A Unified Multi-Task Time Series Model." NeurIPS 2024. [arXiv:2403.00131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00131); [github.com/mims-harvard/UniTS](https://github.com/mims-harvard/UniTS). A single model for forecasting, **classification**, imputation and anomaly detection, with prompt/few-shot capabilities. PyTorch, MIT [K/U].
- **GPT4TS / One Fits All** [K]: Zhou, T., Niu, P., Wang, X., Sun, L., Jin, R. "One Fits All: Power General Time Series Analysis by Pretrained LM." NeurIPS 2023. [arXiv:2302.11939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939). A frozen GPT-2 backbone with trainable embeddings and norms; includes UEA classification.
- **TSPulse (IBM Granite)** [V/K]: The Energy-TSPulse card states it is fine-tuned from `ibm-granite/granite-timeseries-tspulse-r1` [V: [HF card](https://huggingface.co/EnergyFM/energy-tspulse)]. TSPulse is IBM's ultra-light (around 1M params) TSFM for classification, anomaly detection, imputation and search [K/U]. The TSPulse paper arXiv ID was not verified [U].
- **Context: TSFMs on energy forecasting** [V]: A household load forecasting benchmark found TSFMs comparable to Transformers trained from scratch, with Time-MoE, Sundial, Chronos and TimesFM competitive depending on metric. "Benchmarking Time Series Foundation Models for Short-Term Household Electricity Load Forecasting", [arXiv:2410.09487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09487). See also "Exploring Capabilities of Time Series Foundation Models in Building Analytics", [arXiv:2411.08888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08888), and an assessment of TSFMs on building energy management tasks, [arXiv:2506.11250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11250).

### Inferences
- Of the general TSFMs, **MOMENT** (MIT, embeddings, documented classification) and **Mantis** (classification-native, 8M params) are the best-justified frozen-embedding baselines for weekly snippets. Chronos embeddings are a possible extra baseline but are not designed for classification.
- License caveat for "public" initialisations: Moirai weights (CC-BY-NC) may restrict reuse. MIT/Apache models (MOMENT, Chronos, TimesFM, Lag-Llama, Energy-TSPulse) do not.
- CPU feasibility: models under ~20M params (Mantis, TSPulse, Lag-Llama, Chronos-tiny/Bolt-tiny, MOMENT-small) are practical for embedding hundreds of thousands of weekly snippets on CPU. MOMENT-large and Chronos-large are slow. These are inferences from parameter counts, not measured benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No published evaluation of MOMENT, Mantis, Chronos etc. on socio-demographic classification from smart meter data was found.
- Exact parameter counts and licenses for Mantis, UniTS and Moirai versions were not verified this session.

## 3. Energy-specific pretrained / SSL models and use on CER / household attributes

### Takeaway
Energy-specific pretrained models exist but are mostly **forecasting**-oriented (BuildingsBench Transformers, Energy-TTM). The first energy-domain pretrained model that explicitly targets **classification** is **Energy-TSPulse** (EnergyFM, ACM e-Energy 2026; Apache-2.0; around 168k params), evaluated on appliance-usage classification. SSL on smart meter data for **household/demographic** classification is sparse: GridCL (contrastive, limited labels; datasets unnamed), a 2025 self-supervised CNN for demographic inference (venue of modest standing), TransApp (self-supervised transformer pretraining for appliance detection, VLDB 2024), and CNN autoencoder embeddings for socio-demographics (Radovanovic et al. 2026). No paper was found that does contrastive or masked pretraining on **CER** and then classifies **CER survey socio-demographics**. That remains an open niche.

### Cited Findings
- **BuildingsBench** [V]: Emami, P., Sahu, A., Graf, P. "BuildingsBench: A Large-Scale Dataset of 900K Buildings and Benchmark for Short-Term Load Forecasting." NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks. [arXiv:2307.00142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00142); [proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/3f17bf868966df01ca125e5bbc9ee24e-Abstract.html); code and data at [github.com/NREL/BuildingsBench](https://github.com/NREL/BuildingsBench) (PyTorch). Buildings-900K is simulated from the NREL End-Use Load Profiles (around 550K residential, 350K commercial). Transformer-S/M/L are pretrained for zero-shot and fine-tuned STLF. Synthetic pretraining generalises surprisingly well to real commercial buildings. Example: a pretrained and fine-tuned Transformer-L reaches 12.96 NRMSE (commercial) vs 37.21 for the same architecture fine-tuned from random init. Scaling shows a power law with diminishing returns. The real-building evaluation set is understood to include residential smart meter datasets, possibly also Irish data [U, check whether CER/LCL are in the BuildingsBench real set]. Forecasting only; no classification.
- **EnergyFM / Energy-TSPulse and Energy-TTM** [V]: Arjunan, P., Srivastava, N., Kumar, K., Jati, A., Ekambaram, V., Dayama, P. EnergyFM, in *Proc. ACM e-Energy '26*, pp. 556–568, DOI [10.1145/3744255.3798119](https://doi.org/10.1145/3744255.3798119); [HF Energy-TSPulse](https://huggingface.co/EnergyFM/energy-tspulse), [HF Energy-TTM](https://huggingface.co/EnergyFM/energy-ttm). Pretrained on **EnergyBench** (76,217 residential and commercial buildings, 1.26B hourly readings, multiple countries). Energy-TSPulse has about 168k params, is Apache-2.0, comes in general/commercial/residential (512-res) variants, and supports **appliance-usage classification via transfer learning on low-frequency smart meter data**. The card claims performance is competitive with feature-based classifiers (not independently verified). Constituent datasets (incl. CER) are not named on the card [U]. Hourly resolution matches the 7x24 setting. A 512 context means one week (168) needs padding, or about three weeks fit.
- **GridCL** [V]: Zhang, L., Wang, J., Zhang, W., Li, K., Yao, D., Yang, B., Ke, X., Zhao, H., Yao, Y. "GridCL for fine-grained load profiling in smart grids under limited labels." *PLOS ONE* 21(7), 2026. DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0354752](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0354752) ([econpapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0354752)). Contrastive SSL on **daily** load with *conservative* augmentations (small temporal rolling, multiplicative perturbation, energy renormalisation) and a temporal convolutional encoder. Sparse-label user classification accuracy on the pooled set is 0.845 / 0.851 / 0.849 at 10 / 20 / 30% labels (i.e. stable under label scarcity). The datasets are three anonymised cities, **not CER as far as stated**. No fully supervised baseline appears in the abstract.
- **Self-supervised CNN for demographic inference** [V, snippet only, page fetch failed]: an Extrica-published 2025 paper (Aug 12, 2025) proposes a CNN with FFT-based kernels. Self-supervised pretraining predicts temporal/spatial interactions in load signals, and the paper reports improved accuracy, recall and F1 for demographic inference. Dataset not confirmed. [extrica.com/article/24825](https://www.extrica.com/article/24825). Authors and venue quality unverified [U]; treat as weak evidence.
- **TransApp** [V]: Petralia, A., Charpentier, P., Palpanas, T. "ADF & TransApp: A Transformer-Based Framework for Appliance Detection Using Smart Meter Consumption Series." *PVLDB* 17(3):553–562, VLDB 2024. [arXiv:2401.05381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05381). The transformer is "first pretrained in a self-supervised way" and outperforms SOTA TSC classifiers on appliance detection. The search snippet indicates the public dataset is **CER** (ISSDA), with appliance labels from the household questionnaire [V snippet; abstract does not name datasets]. The second dataset is private (EDF) [K/U]. The SSL objective is a masked-reconstruction (MLM-style) pretext task [K/U]. Closest published precedent for "SSL-pretrained transformer on CER, used to classify questionnaire-derived household labels".
- **Appliance detection benchmark on CER** [K/U]: Petralia, A., Charpentier, P., Boniol, P., Palpanas, T. "Appliance Detection Using Very Low-Frequency Smart Meter Time Series." ACM e-Energy 2023. [arXiv:2305.10352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10352). Benchmarks supervised TSC models (ConvNet, ResNet, InceptionTime, ROCKET-family, etc.) on CER and other datasets, using questionnaire-derived appliance labels.
- **CamAL (weak supervision)** [V]: Petralia, A., Boniol, P., Charpentier, P., Palpanas, T. "Few Labels are all you need: A Weakly Supervised Framework for Appliance Localization in Smart-Meter Series." IEEE ICDE 2025, pp. 4386–4399. DOI [10.1109/ICDE65448.2025.00329](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDE65448.2025.00329); [arXiv:2506.05895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05895). Uses only household-level appliance presence labels (an MIL-like setting) with an ensemble of deep classifiers and CAM-based localisation. SOTA supervised NILM needs significantly more labels to match it.
- **CNN autoencoder embeddings for socio-demographics** [V]: Radovanovic, D., Schirl, M., Unterweger, A., Eibl, G. "The Impact of Temporal Granularity on Socio-Demographic Inference from Household Load Profiles." [arXiv:2606.03358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03358) (June 2026, book chapter). 1,589 households over one year (not CER), with 8 attributes at resolutions from 15 min to 7 days. Handcrafted/tsfresh features are **competitive with CNN-autoencoder embeddings**, and XGBoost performs best. Training is on year-round data, testing on arbitrary weeks. Note: this appears to be the project author's own prior work.
- **Multi-prototype representations for household characteristic identification (CER)** [V, snippet only]: a Mendeley Data deposit for work on modelling behavioural ambiguity with multi-prototype representations for household characteristic identification and customer segmentation. It uses Irish CER data (not redistributed; available from ISSDA). [data.mendeley.com/datasets/ybrv5h87k6/1](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/ybrv5h87k6/1). Associated paper not identified [U].
- **CER dataset reference** [V]: CER Smart Metering Project, Electricity Customer Behaviour Trial 2009–2010, over 5,000 Irish homes and businesses; DOI [10.7929/ISSDA/BX59EU](https://doi.org/10.7929/ISSDA/BX59EU) ([b2find](https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/be1df6ba-3124-5b96-9b8b-94434c7a61a9)).
- **Supervised deep baselines on CER socio-demographics (context)** [K]: Wang, Y., Chen, Q., Gan, D., Yang, J., Kirschen, D.S., Kang, C. "Deep Learning-Based Socio-Demographic Information Identification From Smart Meter Data." IEEE Trans. Smart Grid 10(3), 2019, DOI [10.1109/TSG.2018.2805723](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSG.2018.2805723). A CNN on weekly CER load. Beckel, C., Sadamori, L., Staake, T., Santini, S. "Revealing household characteristics from smart meter data." *Energy* 78, 2014, DOI [10.1016/j.energy.2014.10.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2014.10.025) (CER, the CLASS system). Both are supervised; neither uses SSL.

### Inferences
- A public pretrained init for 7x48/7x24 CER-style snippets can credibly be built from (a) Energy-TSPulse (hourly, Apache-2.0, energy-domain), (b) BuildingsBench-pretrained Transformers (forecasting-pretrained, so a different inductive bias), or (c) self-pretraining TS2Vec/TST on a disjoint public smart meter corpus (e.g. LCL London, SmartMeter Energy Consumption, BuildingsBench real data) and then fine-tuning on CER. Option (c) also avoids using target households in pretraining, which matters for a leakage study.
- The Radovanovic et al. finding (AE embeddings are not better than handcrafted features) is a relevant prior: SSL embeddings must be compared against tsfresh/handcrafted + XGBoost baselines, not only deep supervised ones.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that pretrains (contrastive or masked) on CER and evaluates on CER survey socio-demographic targets.
- Whether EnergyBench or BuildingsBench-real includes CER/ISSDA data was not verified. This matters for train/test contamination if those models are used as inits on CER.
- The Extrica self-supervised demographic paper could not be fetched; details unverified.

## 4. Evidence that SSL embeddings help under label scarcity or class imbalance

### Takeaway
General-domain evidence is solid. Liu et al. (ICLR 2022) show SSL representations degrade far less than supervised ones under imbalanced pretraining, and Yang & Xu (NeurIPS 2020) show self-supervised pretraining improves class-imbalanced learning. Time-series evidence is mainly about **label scarcity** (CA-TCC, TS-TCC, TS2Vec few-label results; GridCL on load). No direct time-series study of SSL vs class imbalance on smart meter attributes was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Liu, H., HaoChen, J.Z., Gaidon, A., Ma, T.** "Self-supervised Learning is More Robust to Dataset Imbalance." ICLR 2022 (spotlight). [arXiv:2110.05025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05025); [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6413) [V]. The gap between balanced and imbalanced pretraining is much smaller for SSL than supervised learning, especially out of domain. Their explanation is that SSL learns label-irrelevant but transferable features from frequent classes that help rare classes. They propose a re-weighted regularisation that further improves SSL on imbalanced data. Caveat: vision (ImageNet-LT, CIFAR-LT), not time series.
- **Yang, Y., Xu, Z.** "Rethinking the Value of Labels for Improving Class-Imbalanced Learning." NeurIPS 2020. [arXiv:2006.07529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07529) [K]. Self-supervised pre-training before standard supervised training consistently improves imbalanced classification; semi-supervision with unlabeled data also helps. Vision benchmarks.
- **CA-TCC** (TPAMI 2023) reports that SSL + few-label fine-tuning approaches fully supervised performance with 1–10% labels on HAR/sleep/epilepsy [K]. [arXiv:2208.06616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06616).
- **GridCL** on load data: accuracy is stable from 10% to 30% labels (0.845–0.851) [V]. [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0354752](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0354752).
- **TST** (KDD 2021): unsupervised pretraining improves classification and regression even when it reuses only the labelled training set, with larger gains in low-data regimes [K]. [arXiv:2010.02803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02803).
- **CamAL** (ICDE 2025): weak household-level labels suffice; supervised NILM needs many more labels [V]. [arXiv:2506.05895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05895).

### Inferences
- Household attributes in CER are often strongly imbalanced (e.g. rare heating types, large households). Liu et al. suggest a frozen SSL encoder plus a balanced or reweighted linear/logistic head should be more robust than an end-to-end supervised CNN. This is a testable hypothesis for the project, not a demonstrated smart meter result.
- Report macro-F1 / balanced accuracy and per-class recall to make any imbalance robustness visible.

### Gaps
- No time-series-specific replication of Liu et al. (SSL robustness to imbalance) was found.
- No smart meter study quantifying SSL benefit vs label fraction for socio-demographic targets was found (GridCL is the closest, with unnamed datasets and targets).

## 5. Multiple-instance / set-based household-level aggregation of weekly embeddings

### Takeaway
No smart meter paper was found that applies MIL or set pooling to weekly embeddings for household attribute prediction. The citable building blocks are Deep Sets, attention-based MIL (Ilse et al.), Set Transformer, and time-series MIL (MILLET). In the energy domain, CamAL's weak household-level labels are the closest analogue (appliances, not demographics). Simple mean/majority aggregation of per-week predictions is the de facto practice.

### Cited Findings
- **Attention-based deep MIL** [K]: Ilse, M., Tomczak, J., Welling, M. "Attention-based Deep Multiple Instance Learning." ICML 2018. [arXiv:1802.04712](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04712). Gated-attention pooling over instance embeddings, which gives interpretable instance (week) weights.
- **Deep Sets** [K]: Zaheer, M. et al. "Deep Sets." NeurIPS 2017. [arXiv:1703.06114](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06114).
- **Set Transformer** [K]: Lee, J. et al. "Set Transformer: A Framework for Attention-based Permutation-Invariant Neural Networks." ICML 2019. [arXiv:1810.00825](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00825).
- **MILLET (MIL for time series classification)** [K]: Early, J., Cheung, G.K., Cutajar, K., Xie, H., Kandola, J., Twomey, N. "Inherently Interpretable Time Series Classification via Multiple Instance Learning." ICLR 2024. [arXiv:2311.10049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10049). Treats time points/segments as instances, with PyTorch code.
- **CamAL** [V]: household-level appliance presence labels serve as weak (bag-level) supervision for localisation in smart meter series. [arXiv:2506.05895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05895).
- **Radovanovic et al. 2026** [V]: train on year-round data, test on arbitrary single weeks. This is the per-week inference setting, with no set-level aggregation reported in the abstract. [arXiv:2606.03358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03358).
- **Patent (not smart meter)** [V]: a USPTO document describes an MIL framework for AI household inference models, but its background is smart-TV data. [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12639596).

### Inferences
- For a leakage study, the household-level threat model (an attacker sees k weeks) maps naturally to MIL. An attention-MIL head over frozen weekly SSL embeddings, compared against mean-pooling of embeddings and against averaging per-week probabilities, would be a novel and well-motivated contribution.

### Gaps
- No published MIL/set-based aggregation of weekly load embeddings for demographics was found (searches for "multiple instance" + smart meter + demographic returned nothing directly relevant).

## 6. Standard supervised time-series classification baselines (citable)

### Takeaway
The canonical deep TSC baselines are Wang et al. 2017 (MLP/FCN/ResNet), the Ismail Fawaz et al. 2019 DMKD review (ResNet/FCN best), and InceptionTime (2020). Add ROCKET/MiniRocket as strong non-deep baselines and the 2024 "bake off redux" as the up-to-date benchmark. All have PyTorch implementations (e.g. in `tsai` [K/U] or `aeon` [K/U]), and the supervised deep models are also the usual encoders in smart meter CER classification papers.

### Cited Findings
- **Wang, Z., Yan, W., Oates, T.** "Time Series Classification from Scratch with Deep Neural Networks: A Strong Baseline." IJCNN 2017. [arXiv:1611.06455](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06455); DOI [10.1109/IJCNN.2017.7966039](https://doi.org/10.1109/IJCNN.2017.7966039) [K]. Introduces MLP, FCN (3 conv blocks 128-256-128, GAP) and ResNet (3 residual blocks, 64-128-128) baselines.
- **Ismail Fawaz, H., Forestier, G., Weber, J., Idoumghar, L., Muller, P.-A.** "Deep learning for time series classification: a review." *Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery* 33(4):917–963, 2019. DOI [10.1007/s10618-019-00619-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-019-00619-1); [arXiv:1809.04356](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04356) [K]. Benchmarks 9 deep models on UCR/UEA; ResNet and FCN perform best.
- **Ismail Fawaz, H., Lucas, B., Forestier, G., Pelletier, C., Schmidt, D.F., Weber, J., Webb, G.I., Idoumghar, L., Muller, P.-A., Petitjean, F.** "InceptionTime: Finding AlexNet for time series classification." *DMKD* 34:1936–1962, 2020. DOI [10.1007/s10618-020-00710-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-020-00710-y); [arXiv:1909.04939](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04939) [K]. An ensemble of 5 Inception networks, roughly on par with HIVE-COTE.
- **ROCKET / MiniRocket** [K]: Dempster, A., Petitjean, F., Webb, G.I. "ROCKET: exceptionally fast and accurate time series classification using random convolutional kernels." *DMKD* 34, 2020, [arXiv:1910.13051](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13051). MiniRocket: KDD 2021, [arXiv:2012.08791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08791). Fast, CPU-friendly, strong.
- **Bake off redux** [K]: Middlehurst, M., Schäfer, P., Bagnall, A. "Bake off redux: a review and experimental evaluation of recent time series classification algorithms." *DMKD* 2024. [arXiv:2304.13029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029).
- **Use on CER/appliances** [K/U]: Petralia et al. e-Energy 2023 ([arXiv:2305.10352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10352)) benchmark ResNet, InceptionTime, ROCKET etc. on CER appliance detection. Wang et al. TSG 2019 use a CNN on CER socio-demographics ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSG.2018.2805723)).

### Inferences
- A defensible baseline set for weekly snippets: FCN/ResNet (Wang 2017), InceptionTime, MiniRocket, and tsfresh/handcrafted + XGBoost (per Radovanovic et al. 2026). SSL/pretrained encoders (TS2Vec, TST/PatchTST-SSL, MOMENT, Mantis, Energy-TSPulse) would then serve either as frozen feature extractors with a linear/logistic head, or as initialisation for fine-tuning one of these backbones.

### Gaps
- Exact PyTorch library status (`tsai`, `aeon` deep-learning modules, which partly use TF/Keras in aeon [U]) was not verified this session. The original InceptionTime and Ismail Fawaz review code is Keras/TensorFlow [K], so cite a PyTorch re-implementation (e.g. `tsai`) given the project's PyTorch-only constraint.
